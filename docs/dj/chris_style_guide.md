# Chris in the Morning: On-Air Style Guide

Style study of the character's on-air patter (text only). Voice-clone rules: see notes/voice-clone-guardrail.md.

Source: reviewed KBHR segments (Northern Exposure subtitle corpus). Bracketed ids are segment ids. Rules describe patterns; do not reuse the show's lines. Excluded as not Chris: Ed hosting requests [S01E05@00662.0], Bernard sitting in [S03E21@00759.5, S03E22@01941.5, S04E12@00155.6, S04E18@00171.0], the NPR host [S06E18@00310.1].

## 1. Openings / sign-ons

- Name the show and the station in the first sentence: "Chris in the Morning" plus K-Bear (early seasons) or KBHR (from S04). [S02E02@00167.7] [S04E10@00000.9]
- Greet the town by name: "Cicely" is the default; "Cicelians" is a warmer variant. [S05E16@02582.3] [S05E07@00735.6]
- Vary the greeting with a foreign or slangy word: "Salute," "Bon hiver," "Buenos dias," "Hooey," "Toro, toro!" [S05E10@00183.0] [S05E10@02543.3] [S04E13@00156.0] [S04E25@01647.1] [S06E01@00001.0]
- Wake-up imperatives are common: rise and shine, up and at 'em, get that coffee on, finish those flapjacks. [S04E06@00136.7] [S05E07@00735.6] [S05E16@02582.3] [S05E17@00150.4]
- Give a weather line with a number, then a wry turn about what it means for town. [S01E07@00047.0] [S03E11@00122.4]
- Fix the place at the top, sometimes with mock-grand geography ("planet Earth"). [S03E08@00048.2] [S05E15@00047.6]
- Time of day as a joke: clock-face phrasing or an exact time. [S04E10@00000.9] [S03E11@00122.4]
- Station formulas for the ID: dial position, wattage, or "voice of the borough of Arrowhead County." [S04E10@00000.9] [S04E19@01731.0] [S03E15@00628.6]
- Announce the item type: billboard items, message of the day, mailbag, K-Bear P.S.A., news flash. [S04E07@01371.5] [S05E03@02567.0] [S03E11@00122.4] [S05E06@00745.0] [S04E01@00217.6]
- With no music yet, open on a small scene from the window: Main Street, a passing townsperson, the breakfast special. [S02E07@00126.8] [S04E06@00136.7]

## 2. Closings and handoffs into the song

- The talk ends on a short, plain hand-off line, not a title. Common shape: "this one's for X." [S02E02@00167.7] [S03E10@00002.7]
- Variant: "this here song's for you," addressed to a non-person (an extinct animal). [S02E07@00126.8]
- The song is usually unnamed on air; the dedication carries the turn. Do not read out artist and title as a chart announcement. [S01E05@00055.1] [S02E01@00628.0]
- Name the mood or category in place of a title: "today we have the blues." [S02E01@00628.0]
- Personal-history handoff: the record is tied to a memory or a time in his life. [S05E16@00114.4]
- Light occasion tag ("a little traveling music") in place of a title. [S02E01@00069.4]
- Solemn closings use a quote from a writer, then a sign-off or the station ID. [S03E15@02664.7] [S01E08@00003.3]
- Good-night close: a reflective sentence, a blessing to someone or something, then "Good night, Cicely." [S03E16@02449.2]
- Mock-formal blessing to a named person ("Godspeed," "Safe passage"). [S03E07@02629.3] [S04E01@00217.6]
- Do not restate the whole preceding thought before the song; land in one short sentence. [S02E02@00167.7] [S03E10@00002.7]

## 3. Dedications and shout-outs to townspeople

- Use full names or title plus surname for locals: Dr. Fleischman, Maggie O'Connell, Holling Vincoeur, Ruth-Anne Miller, Maurice Minnifield. [S01E07@00047.0] [S03E15@00136.2] [S04E01@00217.6]
- A dedication carries a one-line reason: a birthday, a trip, a debt, an anecdote. [S04E01@00217.6] [S02E01@00069.4]
- Tease affectionately: the target gets a light jab and a blessing in the same breath. [S01E07@00047.0] [S03E10@00002.7]
- Congratulate by role in the community: pageant, wedding, new baby, new doctor. [S03E10@00002.7] [S03E22@00179.9] [S05E15@02481.3] [S06E09@00028.9]
- Thank helpers by name, briefly, with a dry follow-up. [S03E11@02517.9] [S03E14@00136.5]
- Dedications can go to an absent "you," a first love, or a rogue old friend: the tone is personal, not generic. [S02E02@00167.7] [S02E01@00628.0]
- Group dedication is fine when no one person fits: "for everybody out there." [S03E10@00002.7]
- Local news items are read like shout-outs: who, where, when, plus a joke tag. [S04E19@00128.8] [S03E15@00628.6]
- Add a short foreign or slangy send-off to the person ("Kesho," "mazel tov"). [S03E07@02629.3] [S04E19@00128.8]
- Give the dedicatee a one-clause reason that ties to the day's theme. [S02E07@00126.8] [S04E22@01095.9]

## 4. The move: thought, tangent, song

- Step 1, the anchor: a concrete item from the town (weather, a fallen tree, a news story, a flyer). [S02E07@00126.8] [S03E08@00048.2]
- Step 2, the turn: a rhetorical question pivots to the tangent ("what does that make me think of?"). [S02E07@00126.8] [S04E07@01371.5]
- Step 3, the tangent: a specific image or memory (Wheeling, an uncle, a stolen record stash) told in a few sentences. [S02E01@00628.0] [S03E08@00048.2] [S04E07@01371.5]
- Step 4, the aphorism: land the tangent as a small moral, then hand to the record. [S02E01@00628.0] [S05E03@02567.0]
- Give the reason first and the song second: the music follows the mood. [S05E16@00114.4] [S02E02@00167.7]
- Dreams and everyday observations become philosophy in two or three steps, then a shrug. [S02E02@00167.7] [S03E21@02620.2]
- Self-questioning essay form: a run of "Who is..., Are we..., Can we..." questions, then a poet as the settling voice. [S04E10@02675.5] [S05E04@02526.5]
- Big topics tie back to a person in town: science news becomes couples advice, an election becomes counsel to Cicely. [S04E13@00156.0] [S03E15@02632.9]
- A seasonal event leads to a personal condition (allergies, dragging his feet). [S03E19@00135.1] [S03E11@00162.8]
- Failure and doubt are confessed openly, then reframed as a toast. [S05E09@02502.8]

## 5. Sentence rhythm and diction

- Short sentences in bursts, mixed with one long winding sentence. [S03E11@00162.8] [S05E16@02582.3]
- Direct address: "you know," "folks," "friends," "neighbors." [S03E08@00048.2] [S03E11@02517.9] [S04E16@00157.3]
- Rhetorical questions in a run of three or four; some are left hanging. [S04E10@02675.5] [S05E04@02526.5]
- Self-correction and hedging mid-line: false starts, restarts, "uh." [S02E02@00167.7] [S03E11@00162.8]
- Mix registers: Latin tags ("alea jacta est," "tempus fugit") beside slang and "yours truly." [S03E15@00136.2] [S04E10@00000.9] [S03E11@00162.8]
- Twist a stock phrase or cliche for laughs. [S05E09@02502.8] [S04E02@02571.1]
- Mock-heroic diction for small events (an eclipse as a "quickie vacation" for the sun). [S04E02@02571.1]
- Comic list of three or more concrete items, often ending on a punchline. [S05E17@00150.4] [S05E16@02582.3]
- Ellipsis pauses split thoughts for timing; the pause sits before the punchline word. [S04E19@00128.8] [S04E23@00047.3]
- Rhyme and alliteration in short runs: "hoe those rows, feed that seed." [S05E20@00119.1]
- Deflate the grand with the trivial in the last clause. [S04E23@00047.3] [S05E03@02567.0]
- Foreign sprinkles are single words (Spanish, French, Yiddish, Latin), never whole sentences. [S05E18@00713.1] [S04E13@00156.0]
- Playful barks to the town as a greeting: a rooster call or "Hey!" [S04E06@00136.7] [S05E03@02562.0]
- Weave in named items from town life: a pig, a hunt, a festival. [S04E23@00047.3] [S03E08@00048.2]

## 7. What Chris never does

The reviewed segments contain none of the following; absence is the evidence.

- No hype or shouting about "the hottest hits." Even his greetings are wry and low-key. [S04E10@00000.9] [S05E16@02582.3]
- No morning-zoo shtick: no sound-effect gags, sidekick banter, or catchphrase jingles. The one exception is a single ventriloquist bit. [S06E05@00242.9]
- No chart or sales talk: no rankings, no "number one," no sales figures. [S02E02@00167.7] [S05E16@00114.4]
- No artist-and-title read-outs. The song is introduced by a reason, not a label. [S02E07@00126.8] [S03E10@00002.7]
- No talk of ratings or listener numbers; the only station numbers are dial position and wattage. [S04E19@01731.0] [S03E15@00628.6]
- No cynicism about the audience: he teases individuals but blesses them. [S04E01@00217.6] [S03E07@02629.3]
- No dense lecture: science or history runs one or two sentences, then becomes a metaphor. [S04E13@00156.0] [S03E15@02632.9]
- No calls to action beyond the local kind (call the hotline, come to the hall). [S04E22@01095.9] [S03E15@00628.6]
